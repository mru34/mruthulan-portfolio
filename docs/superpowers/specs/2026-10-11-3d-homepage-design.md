# 3D homepage redesign: design spec

Date: 11 October 2026 · Branch: `redesign-3d` · Status: waiting for Mruthulan's review

## Goal

The homepage should feel premium and eye-catching, like an Apple product page: real 3D objects that move with the scroll and the mouse, very few words, and every project understood at a glance. Today it reads as crowded and generic.

The content stays the same: same projects, same results, same rules about what is published. Only the design and the motion change, and the homepage loses most of its sentences.

## Audience and success

- **Recruiters and hiring managers** for a year-long software engineering internship, 2027/2028. Often on a phone, from LinkedIn. They should know within seconds who this is, see every project with its result, and find the résumé, LinkedIn, GitHub and email without looking.
- **Everyone else** (friends, classmates, people from events) should find it striking enough to scroll to the end and share.

It works if:
1. Scrolling top to bottom shows all five projects, one by one, each with its name, one line and its result.
2. Next to the references (Apple product pages, landonorris.com, lusion.co, linear.app), the 3D looks like it belongs in the same league, not like generated filler. Mruthulan judges this at the review gate (see Build order).
3. The first screen appears at least as fast as it does today, and scrolling stays smooth on a mid-range phone, or the phone falls back to stills without anyone noticing a problem.

## Rules carried over (unchanged)

- Every page arrives finished. Nothing spins, types itself or fades in on load. Motion only answers the visitor: scroll position, pointer, press.
- Motion never hides content at rest.
- No stock or generated imagery. Every screen in the 3D shows a real capture or a crop of one; every photo is real.
- Nothing unconfirmed is published. No claim is added that the case pages don't already make.
- No visible reading text smaller than 16px; on touch screens every link and button is at least 44px tall.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` turns all movement off.
- Native scrolling. The wheel, trackpad and touch are never blocked.
- Static site on GitHub Pages, generated by `design/build_site.py`. Never edit the generated HTML.
- The organisers' logos only name where each event was held.
- Commits are credited to Mruthulan only.

## The flow (how it should feel, like Apple and the reference sites)

Apple's product pages, landonorris.com and lusion.co share a rhythm, and the page follows it:

- **Big moments, then calm.** A loud moment (a 3D object doing something) is followed by a quiet one (big type on dark, or photos), so neither gets tiring. Here: Hero (loud) → "Five projects" (quiet) → five chapters (loud) → Awards (quiet, real photos) → About (quiet) → Reach out (loud again: the board).
- **One idea per screen.** Each screen has one thing to look at and at most one sentence.
- **Every chapter has three beats, tied to the scroll** (Apple's reveal, show, payoff):
  1. **Reveal:** the project name arrives alone, huge, in its own typeface, while the 3D element comes in.
  2. **Show:** the element does its thing, and the one line appears under the name.
  3. **Payoff:** the result and *Case study →* appear. This is the key pose the snap settles on, and everything is visible in it.
- **Type does the work.** Headings are very large and very short, with lots of empty dark space around them. Small text is rare.
- **Motion has weight and follows the visitor.** Nothing plays by itself.

### 2. Hero: the 3D name board

- The whole first screen is the name as a real 3D split-flap board: MRUTHULAN in gold letters on the top row, SENTHIL NATHAN in white below. On phones: three rows (MRUTHULAN / SENTHIL / NATHAN).
- Under it, three icon links: LinkedIn, GitHub, Résumé. Nothing else: no eyebrow, no tagline, no results.
- Each tile is a real split-flap unit: a top and a bottom half, a matte painted-metal body, a fine split line and visible hinge pins. The letter is printed on the flaps in Archivo.
- **First frame:** the name is already showing. A pre-rendered still of the exact first frame is the page's first paint; the live 3D replaces it in the same frame once it has drawn, with no fade and no visible change.
- **Mouse:** the board tilts up to about 4° toward the cursor, and a point of light follows the cursor so a glint slides across the flaps. The flap under the cursor turns through a few letters and lands back on its own.
- **Click or tap:** a flip ripples across the whole name from that tile and lands back on the name.
- **Scroll:** the board tilts back and rises away as the camera moves forward, handing over to the fly-through.
- The `<h1>` stays real text for screen readers and search ("Mruthulan Senthil Nathan").

### 3. Fly-through: "Five projects"

About one screen of scrolling, all tied to the scroll position, so scrolling up plays it backwards.

- The camera moves forward through the dark. A sparse field of fine gold dust (a few hundred points) streams past to give a sense of speed. If the dust reads as filler at the review gate, it is removed.
- "Five projects." sits large in the middle (HTML text, Bricolage) and moves only with the scroll. It is readable at every point.
- The flight ends on the opening pose of chapter 01, SignalBridge.

### 4. The project chapters

Order: 01 SignalBridge, 02 Boss Breaker, 03 Better Call Bhai, 04 MEANT, 05 KnowCad. The exact words for each chapter are in "Every word on the homepage" below.

**What every chapter shares: the information block.** The number (01–05), the project name (in its own typeface and colour), the one-line pitch, the result, and *Case study →*. Nothing else in words. The block's position changes from chapter to chapter (left, right, bottom-left and so on) so the page never repeats itself, but it always holds the same four things in the same order, so a recruiter knows where to look. Clicking the name or the 3D element also opens the case study; the name keeps its view transition into the case page title.

**One by one.** Each chapter is pinned for about 1.5 screens of scrolling, and its 3D element is driven by the scroll position within it. When scrolling stops (about 150 ms with no input), the page glides to the nearest chapter's key pose, so each normal scroll lands on the next project. Snapping never fights active scrolling, never blocks the wheel or touch, and is off with reduced motion. A slim rail on the side marks the five chapters and the current one. It is a progress marker, not a set of jump links.

### Writing rules (so nothing sounds AI-written)

- Say it the way you'd say it out loud to a friend: plain words, short sentences.
- Facts instead of adjectives: names, places, numbers, results.
- Banned words: innovative, seamless, cutting-edge, passionate, empower, leverage, elevate, journey, crafted, unlock, robust, delve.
- No slogan tricks: no "not X, but Y", no three-item lists for rhythm, no rhetorical questions, no "Imagine…", no "The result: …" reveals, no dashes used for drama, no exclamation marks.
- Jargon only where a recruiter expects it (SWE, the stack). Otherwise spell it out ("AAC users" becomes "people who can't rely on their speech").
- Every claim is already true on the case page or in the repository.

## Craft rules (so it looks real)

- **One studio.** One lighting setup for everything: a soft key light from the top left, a dim fill, and reflections from one environment map. The project colour appears only as a rim of light on edges, never as a glow.
- **No:** bloom, lens flares, neon, chromatic aberration, purple-blue gradients, floating random shapes, hologram UI, glossy plastic on everything, objects with no shadow, linear motion.
- **Real materials:** matte painted metal (flaps), brushed aluminium (laptop, tablet), glass (phone screens, barber pole), chrome (pole caps), paper with grain (prints, drafting sheets, newsprint).
- **Real proportions:** devices built to real-world sizes (a 14-inch laptop, a 6.1-inch phone, an 11-inch tablet), with bevelled edges and thickness.
- **Grounded:** soft contact shadows under every object.
- **Motion with weight:** everything settles on springs (eases in, settles), never at constant speed.
- **Words are HTML.** No text is drawn in the 3D except what is in a real capture and the flap letters.
- **Devices are generic.** Built in code, they look like a laptop, a phone and a tablet, not like a specific brand's product.

## How it is built

**Approach: hybrid.** One WebGL canvas (three.js) for the 3D: the boards, the fly-through and the five chapter elements. Everything with words is normal HTML on top. Awards and the portrait use CSS 3D.

**Libraries,** saved into the repo at pinned versions (no CDN, no build step), loaded with an import map:
- three.js (ES module build, plus `RoundedBoxGeometry` and `RoomEnvironment` from its addons)
- GSAP with ScrollTrigger (free for commercial use since 2025) for pinning, scrubbing and the snap

**One stage.** `js/3d/stage.js` owns the renderer, one fixed full-screen canvas behind the page, the camera and the studio lighting. It renders only when something changes (scroll, pointer, a spring still settling) and sleeps otherwise, so nothing moves by itself and phones don't burn battery.

**Each element is its own module** with the same shape: `load()` fetches its textures, `update(progress)` poses it for a scroll position between 0 and 1, `pointer(x, y)` and `press()` handle the mouse and taps, `dispose()` frees it.

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `js/3d/stage.js` | Renderer, canvas, camera, studio light, render-on-demand loop |
| `js/3d/board.js` | The split-flap board (hero and ending) |
| `js/3d/devices.js` | Laptop, phone, tablet and partner display, built in code |
| `js/3d/flythrough.js` | The camera move and the dust |
| `js/3d/chapters/signalbridge.js` and four more | One file per chapter |
| `js/scroll.js` | ScrollTrigger pins, scrubbing, the snap and the rail |
| `js/fallback.js` | Decides 3D or stills; the frame-time watchdog |

`js/script.js` keeps the viewer, analytics hooks, copy button, menu and button effects; the spin board and name-docking code are removed.

**Assets.** A new `design/build_3d_assets.py` makes the screen textures from the raw captures in `portfolio-tools/card-shots/raw` (outside the repo) and the MEANT captures: resized (laptop and tablet screens 2048 px wide, phones 1024 px tall), cropped where a chapter needs a crop, saved as WebP in `assets/3d/`. The raw files stay outside the repo.

**Stills.** A script in `portfolio-tools` (Node and puppeteer-core, as for the existing card shots) opens the page in headless Chrome, poses each element at its key moment, and saves stills to `assets/3d/stills/` for desktop and phone layouts. Stills are the hero's first paint, and the whole fallback.

**Generator.** `build_home()` in `design/build_site.py` is rewritten for the new sections, from the existing project data (`PROJECTS`, `AWARDS`, `MEDIA`) plus a new `CHAPTERS` table (number, layout side, result, captures and still for each). `header()` gets the icon links. The README's Design section is rewritten to describe the new homepage, and the `spin_result` line comes out of its Analytics section.

## Loading and speed

- First paint: the HTML, the CSS, the Archivo font and the hero still. three.js, GSAP and the hero board load right after, as modules, without blocking.
- Each chapter's textures and font load when the visitor is within about 1.5 screens of it.
- Pixel ratio capped at 2 on desktops and 1.5 on phones.
- Budgets: the 3D libraries plus the site's own 3D code at most about 260 KB compressed; each chapter's textures at most about 400 KB.

## Fallbacks

The 3D runs only with WebGL 2, without reduced motion, without Save-Data, and (where the browser reports it) on devices with at least 4 GB of memory. Otherwise, and if JavaScript is off, every element is its still image next to the same information block. Nothing moves, nothing is missing, every link works.

A watchdog checks frame times while the visitor scrolls. If frames average over 40 ms for two seconds it lowers the resolution; if they are still slow, it switches to stills in place.

## Accessibility

- All words are real HTML. The canvas is hidden from screen readers; each element's still carries the alt text from `data/media.json`.
- *Attack* (Boss Breaker), the four replies (MEANT) and the stacks (Awards) are real buttons with visible focus, usable from the keyboard. Results are announced politely.
- The rail and snap are off under reduced motion; the flaps change without turning.

## Analytics

Removed: `spin_result`. Unchanged: `case_open`, `resume_click`, `proof_post_click`, `press_viewer_open`, `press_article_click`, `deck_open`, `contact_copy_email`, `contact_open_mail_app`, `contact_open_linkedin`, `contact_open_github`. No new events.

## Testing

- `python design/build_site.py`, then `python design/check_site.py --unchanged` and `python design/check_site.py .` pass (generated pages match, no broken links, images or anchors).
- Smoothness: headless Chrome with a 4× slower CPU scrolls the whole page at desktop (1440×900) and phone (390×844, pixel ratio 3) sizes; slow frames (over 33 ms) stay under 5% of frames, or the phone run ends on stills.
- First paint: the hero appears no later than today's homepage on the same throttled run; layout shift stays at 0.
- Fallbacks: forced reduced motion, forced no-WebGL and JavaScript off each show every chapter's still, information block and links.
- Keyboard: Tab reaches every link and button in order, including *Attack* and the MEANT replies.
- Screenshots of each chapter at its key pose, desktop and phone, attached to the pull request.

## Risks

- **Weight on phones:** stills fallback, lazy chapters, capped resolution, the watchdog.
- **It still looks cheap:** the craft rules and the review gate after step 2, before most of the work.
- **The snap feels like being held:** it only acts after scrolling stops, it is gentle, and it can be removed without touching anything else.
- **Five extra typefaces look messy:** only the names use them.
- **MEANT can't be run locally:** fall back to the existing `meant-shot-*` images and the partner display "Give me a moment" capture, with the pick showing the reply on the tablet side instead.
