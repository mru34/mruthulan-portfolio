# Mruthulan's portfolio

Personal portfolio for Senthil Nathan Mruthulan at [mruthulan.com](https://mruthulan.com). A static site (HTML, CSS and JavaScript) hosted on GitHub Pages. The pages are generated by one Python script; there is no other build step.

## Design: Night Edition

One dark ground, off-white type, set a size larger on laptops and up (19px reading text) than on phones. Each kind of text has one face: condensed Archivo capitals for the name, titles and project names; Bricolage Grotesque for big statements (the hero line, leads); Geist for reading text and labels; JetBrains Mono only for proof (routes, file names, the stack, figures like S$3,000). Emphasis is weight and gold, never italics. The signature is a split-flap departures board. Titles are gold (#E8B64C; a deeper #8A6400 on the newsprint panel). Project colour appears in small marks, on the project in focus, and on its case page, where the name and the section headings take the project's colour. The colours: SignalBridge teal, MEANT sky blue, Better Call Bhai yellow, KnowCad violet, Boss Breaker orange.

The homepage reads like a product page: the name, then five projects one by one, then Awards, About and the ending board. It has very few words; every one of them is listed in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-11-3d-homepage-design.md`.

- **Header** is sticky on every page, with Work, Awards and About. On the homepage the gold MRUTHULAN logo is hidden while the name fills the screen and appears once you scroll past it.
- **Hero**: the name as a 3D split-flap board (MRUTHULAN in gold, SENTHIL NATHAN in white; three rows on phones), then LinkedIn, GitHub and Résumé. The board leans toward the mouse with a warm glint under the pointer, a flap flickers under the mouse and lands back, a press ripples the whole name, and scrolling on tilts it back and away. The page paints a still of the board rendered from the same scene (`assets/3d/stills/`), and the live board takes over from it unseen.
- **Five projects.**: the title moves with the scroll while gold dust streams past, as if the page carries you forward.
- **Chapters** (`#work`, `#work-<id>`): SignalBridge, Boss Breaker, Better Call Bhai, MEANT, KnowCad. Each holds the screen for about one and a half screens of scrolling: the name arrives first (in the project's own typeface, `assets/fonts/name-*.woff2`), then the one line, then the result and Case study. When scrolling stops near a chapter's payoff ahead, the page glides onto it (never back). A rail on the side marks the chapter you are in. SignalBridge is the bridge: the youth's real message lifts off the phone, stops at the real consent card, which turns to "Worker notified", and lands on the laptop as the handoff brief. The other four show their pictures until their own 3D is built.
- **Awards** (`#awards`) are three cards side by side, as on the live site: the organisers' logos (Dell InnovateDash, Dell Technologies and Singapore Polytechnic, where it was held; Dell InnovateFest, Dell Technologies; the Autodesk hackathon, Autodesk; the official files in `assets/logos/`, shown in white, linking nowhere), the event's photos with a caption and arrows (arrow keys and swipe too; a tap opens the viewer), the placing, the event, the project and my LinkedIn post. While a card is in view and nobody is pointing at or using it, its photos move on slowly by themselves; using a card's photos stops that card. On phones the three cards are a swipe row with arrows, dots and a counter.
- **Press** is on MEANT's case page (`meant.html#press`): the Tamil Murasu page of 28 September 2026 (page 8), which featured MEANT, on a newsprint-coloured panel with one line, Read in Tamil, and the page itself, which opens in the full-screen viewer (buttons, wheel, pinch, double-click, drag, arrow keys, Reset, Original, Close; Escape closes and focus returns). MEANT's "Featured in Tamil Murasu" button jumps there.
- **About**: the portrait and three facts: Study, Leadership, Stack.
- **Ending** (`#contact`): a flap board, OPEN TO A SWE INTERNSHIP / 2027/28, then "Reach out" with Gmail, LinkedIn and GitHub, and the address with a Copy button.
- **Case pages** open with the name at display size, a one-line headline, a short summary, the actions and a facts row (role, result, stack, partner, status). SignalBridge, Better Call Bhai and Boss Breaker then open on "What I built" (`FEATURED` in the generator): the real product screenshot beside a short list whose lines are linked hotspots. Pointing at a line (or tapping or focusing it) zooms the screenshot to that part with a spotlight, and where there is nothing on screen to show (API routes, tests, database tables) a card opens with the real names from the repository: SignalBridge from my commits to the public repository, Better Call Bhai from its private repository (a solo build), Boss Breaker from its public repository (a solo build). The other case pages open on their lead picture. Then a few sections (a handoff flow, a turn, a before and after, a funnel, a loop) and Previous · All work · Next. Role and result are stated once, in the facts row. Where a repository backs it, a "My part" or "In the code" section lists specific contributions as short cards, each with a line saying where the evidence lives. A thin line at the top shows reading progress. On phones, SignalBridge, Better Call Bhai and Boss Breaker open on just the phone from their scene, with its own caption (`PHONE_SCENES`; the crop is saved once as `*-case-phone.webp`), and evidence pictures sit one to a row on every screen so their text can be read.

### Motion

The 3D is one fixed three.js canvas behind the page (`js/3d/stage.js`): the page lays out every box, and the 3D fills them, so the stills line up with the live frames. It draws only when something changes. It runs only with WebGL 2, without reduced motion or Save-Data, and on devices with at least 4 GB of memory; otherwise, without JavaScript, or if frames stay slow while scrolling, every 3D box shows its still. `?mode=stills` and `?mode=3d` force either.

Native scrolling only (in-page links glide to their section). Every page arrives finished: nothing spins, spells itself out or fades in on load. Motion follows the scroll position and never hides content: photos drift slightly inside their frames, and the name board tilts back and away as you scroll past it. Moving between the home page and a case page uses cross-document view transitions where the browser supports them: the project name travels into the case title.

The page answers the pointer. Award photos, case-page screenshots and the portrait tilt toward the mouse (no glare); on touch they tilt toward the finger while pressed. Buttons lean a few pixels toward the mouse and fill with colour from the side the pointer came in (outline buttons fill white, solid ones with the project's colour or amber). The newspaper lifts off its panel under the mouse. Flaps on the name board flicker under the mouse, and award photos move on slowly while nobody is using them.

Smoothness: everything that moves on hover or scroll animates only transform and opacity, which the browser's compositor draws without redoing layout or paint, so motion keeps pace with any screen (60, 120 or 144 Hz) and runs at the same speed on all of them. Pointer effects update at most once per screen frame, however fast the mouse reports, and measure without forcing layout; the tilt writes its transform directly (no inherited custom properties). Shadows are drawn once and faded in, nothing blurs what is behind it live, and the 3D draws only when something changes, its springs and flap turns all in one frame-synced loop. Featured screenshots and award photos are decoded before they are shown. The case-page reading bar is tied to the scroll by CSS where the browser supports it (script otherwise). Measured in headless Chrome with a 4× slower CPU and a 250 Hz mouse, this cut slow frames while hovering a result photo from 45 to 4, page re-layouts under the newspaper magnifier from 800 to 3, and re-layouts while scrolling the page from 306 to 77. With the 3D homepage, the same 4× slower CPU scrolls the whole page with 0.2% slow frames on a laptop and on a phone (`npm run perf`), and the first paint on a slow 4G phone is 1716 ms, against 1972 ms for the page before it (`tests/browser/firstpaint.mjs`).

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` all of this is off (only colour changes remain): every 3D element is its still, the page does not glide onto a chapter, award photos stay put, the hotspot view changes without moving, and nothing moves by itself.

### Rules the design depends on

- No stock imagery. Every photo and screenshot is real and captioned; screenshots use fictional or test data.
- Nothing unconfirmed is published. Where wording is not confirmed it is left out rather than guessed.
- The KnowCad repository and the Autodesk materials are private, so that page has no code link and no product screenshots.
- The MEANT repository is linked nowhere.
- The Better Call Bhai testimonial is the owner's own WhatsApp message, emoji removed and one sentence left out, marked with an ellipsis (`data/content.json` records what was cut).
- The phone number on the résumé is never published.
- The organisers' logos only name where each event was held: no links, no suggestion of endorsement.
- No visible reading text is smaller than 16px.
- On touch screens every standalone link and button is at least 44px tall.
- The SignalBridge event is "Dell InnovateDash 2026" (the finals signage and the proof post); the SignalBridge page notes that Singapore Polytechnic's feature calls it "SP InnovateDash 2026".

## Editing and rebuilding

All page content lives in `design/build_site.py` (project facts, case-page sections, home page) plus `data/content.json` (the About paragraph and the testimonial) and `data/media.json` (image files, alt text, captions and sizes). Never edit the generated HTML.

```
python design/build_site.py
```

This writes `index.html`, the five case pages, `privacy.html` and `404.html`, makes 800px-wide copies of large images for phones (`*-800.webp`; originals are untouched), and stamps the CSS and JS links. It needs Python 3 and Pillow.

The 3D's pieces are made by scripts:

```
python design/build_3d_assets.py     # screen textures and crops from the real captures → assets/3d/
python design/build_name_fonts.py    # each project's name font → assets/fonts/name-*.woff2
npm run vendor:three                 # three.js, bundled and minified → js/vendor/three/
npm run stills -- hero signalbridge  # stills rendered from the live scenes (serve the site on :8000 first)
```

Tests: `npm install` and `python -m pip install -r design/requirements-dev.txt` once, then `npm test` (pure logic), `python -m pytest tests -q` (what the generator writes; build first), and `npm run smoke` (the page in a browser; serve on :8000). `npm run perf -- desk|phone` checks smoothness.

After changing only `css/` or `js/`, run `python design/stamp_assets.py` so browsers fetch the new files instead of a cached copy.

Run `python -m http.server 8000` in this folder and open `http://localhost:8000`.

## Publishing and domain

Every push to `main` runs `.github/workflows/pages.yml`, which checks the site and publishes it with GitHub Actions (Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions). It rebuilds the pages and fails if they differ from the committed HTML (so the generated pages are never edited by hand), then collects only what the site serves (the HTML pages, `css/`, `js/`, `assets/`, `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`) and fails if any link, image or #anchor on them points at nothing. The generator, `design/`, `data/` and notes like this README stay in the repository and off the website. Pull requests run the same checks without publishing. Run them locally with `python design/check_site.py --unchanged` after a build, and `python design/check_site.py .` for the links.

The custom domain is `mruthulan.com` (set in the Pages settings; the `CNAME` file records it). The Namecheap DNS zone should have GitHub Pages' four apex A records and a `www` CNAME to `mru34.github.io`.

## Analytics

The Google Analytics 4 measurement ID is configured in `js/analytics.js`. The question appears as a slim bar once a visitor scrolls into the page, and the tag loads only after they choose **Yes**; the choice is stored in that browser and can be changed on the privacy page. After consent the site sends `case_open`, `resume_click`, `proof_post_click` (with the project), `press_viewer_open`, `press_article_click`, `contact_copy_email`, `contact_open_mail_app`, `contact_open_linkedin` and `contact_open_github`. No names, email addresses or free text are sent.

## Link previews

Each case study shares with its own 1200×630 card (`assets/og/<page>.jpg`); the home page, privacy and 404 use `assets/og/home.jpg`. The cards are drawn from the site's fonts and pictures by `portfolio-tools/card-shots/og.mjs` (outside the repo); re-run it after changing a project's picture, wording or result, then convert the PNGs to JPG and rebuild. Each `og:image` URL carries a hash of the file, so LinkedIn fetches a changed card again. `assets/og-card.jpg` is kept, with the home card's content, for links shared before.

## Fonts

Archivo, Bricolage Grotesque, Geist and JetBrains Mono are served from `assets/fonts/` (latin subsets of the Google Fonts variable files, OFL; see `assets/fonts/LICENSES.txt`). `css/styles.css` declares them and every page preloads Archivo, the name and board font, so the first paint never waits on another domain.

## Résumé

The résumé is built from text by `python design/build_resume.py` (one A4 page; needs reportlab), which writes `assets/Mruthulan-Senthil-Nathan-Resume.pdf` and the same file at the old address, `assets/Senthil-Nathan-Mruthulan-Resume.pdf`, so older links still work. Every line matches what the site says, so change both together. The phone number is never published, so it is not in the script; add it to your own copy for applications.
