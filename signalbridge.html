CASE STUDY / SP INNOVATEDASH 2026 CHAMPION

SignalBridge.

Making every handoff count when a young person needs support after hours.

Built for a challenge from the Singapore Children’s Society, SignalBridge helps youth workers maintain context and continuity across conversations without taking decisions away from people.

RECOGNITIONSP InnovateDash 2026 Champion
MY FOCUSYouth UX, integrations and testing
STACKNext.js · FastAPI · PostgreSQL

01 / THE PROBLEM

Support should carry context forward.

Youth workers can receive difficult messages across different platforms, sometimes after their shift ends. By morning, important context may be scattered, and the young person may have to repeat their story.

Our team set out to make that handoff clearer and more humane. The system gathers relevant signals, but workers remain responsible for judgement and follow-up.

02 / THE PRODUCT

One place for a careful handoff.

SignalBridge brings support conversations together, offers an after-hours companion, and prepares a brief that is shared only with the young person’s consent. It also helps staff prioritise risk signals and monitor workload.

The safety model combines rules with AI assistance and a fallback path when AI is unavailable. It is designed to support human decisions, not to act as an autonomous counsellor.

03 / MY CONTRIBUTION

From interface to integration.

I worked on the youth-facing experience and consent flow, connected frontend work to backend APIs, contributed to the Discord integration, and wrote automated tests to make the handoff flow more reliable.

Working to a hackathon deadline meant choosing what mattered most, testing quickly, and keeping the team aligned as the product came together.

04 / THE RESULT

Recognised by SP.

SignalBridge won the SP InnovateDash 2026 final presentation. Singapore Polytechnic featured the team and the project’s purpose. The win earned our team a place at Dell InnovateFest, where we later built a separate product, MEANT.
